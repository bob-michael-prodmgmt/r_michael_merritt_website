02

Three places where I read my own results against myself

Fitting a model is table stakes. The harder skill is estimating how much of a result is real. These three are the clearest evidence of it.

Hindsight, priced
13.08 pp

I built an oracle-gap rank statistic to measure what peeking at the test window would have been worth. Selecting hyperparameters with hindsight gained 2.65 score points on average across five model families and 13.08 points of R²OS on the regression tree.

The conclusion follows directly: any backtest that selects and evaluates on the same sample has to deduct that amount before it reports anything.

Identification, tested
Same MSE, different κ, θ

A Heston calibration converged to MSE 0.2457. Re-running the fit through an independent Carr–Madan Fourier engine reproduced identical error from a different parameter set.

That is not two successes. It is one underdetermined problem, and the analysis says so. The Bates extension — eight parameters against five strikes — was flagged as underdetermined on the same grounds.

A good fit, distrusted
R² = 0.999

A log consumption–income regression returned R² of 0.999 and a Durbin–Watson of 0.244. I reported the R² as a spurious-regression warning rather than a result, and defended the low DW as expected under cointegration.

The error-correction term then came back at −0.0473 with p = 0.082. I named that a real problem with the model instead of rounding it into significance.

What actually transfers

  • Validation design under serial dependenceRejected k-fold as invalid for dependent data and built expanding-window, one-step-ahead refit schemes with per-year folds so that standard errors exist at all. Scalers, PCA and k-means refit inside the loop, never on the full sample.
  • Inference for nested forecastsClark–West and Pesaran–Timmermann implemented by hand rather than taken from a library, plus circular block bootstrap with a six-month block to respect autocorrelation when comparing Sharpe ratios.
  • Discretization controlFull-truncation Euler for the Heston variance process, the −λk compensator in the Merton SDE, Feller conditions computed rather than cited, and Monte Carlo discrepancies expressed in standard errors instead of absolute terms.
  • Multiple-testing accountingConfiguration counts reported alongside results — roughly 90 configurations and 12 strategies on one 63-month sample in one study, 141 configurations in another — so a reader can discount the best result appropriately.
  • Benchmark disciplineA parameter-free ten-month moving-average rule beat every tuned model in two separate studies. It stayed in the write-up both times, because leaving it out would overstate the position.
  • Regularization as a prior, not a knobSelection by the one-standard-error rule with an explicit complexity ordering per family, which changed the chosen model in four of seven cases and produced a better out-of-sample Sharpe than the arg-max choice.
  • ReproducibilityFixed seeds, local data caches with documented synthetic fallback, pinned library versions, CSV and JSON result exports, recorded runtimes.

Machine learningModel selection431 monthly observations, 1990–2025

Hyperparameter optimization under serial dependence

Library defaults are tuned for independent data. Equity return panels are not independent. How much of a model's apparent skill is really a tuning artifact — and how much would you gain by cheating?

Design
  • Target 431 monthly observations built from daily index, volatility and Treasury series, with 12 month-end predictors.
  • Split 119-month training seed, 96-month validation in 8 annual folds, 216-month held-out test.
  • Scheme expanding window, one-step-ahead monthly refit, citing Bergmeir on why k-fold is inadmissible here.
  • Search 141 configurations across 7 families; grid, random, and Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization compared under a 20-evaluation budget.
  • Selection one-standard-error rule with a stated complexity ordering per family.

Every selected configuration landed at or near maximum regularization — ridge α = 10,000, a depth-1 tree, PCR on a single component, an 8×4 network with α = 100. The data supports far less model than the defaults assume.

Bayesian search matched or beat the alternatives in five of seven families, with the right boundary drawn on that claim: in single-hyperparameter spaces all three optimizers converge, so optimizer choice only matters under a constrained budget.

Regime modelingHidden Markov models5,472 trading days, 2004–2026

Regime detection and tactical asset rotation

Can a hidden Markov model fitted to volatility log-changes identify market states cleanly enough to drive an allocation rule — and does the edge survive when you change the number of states?

Design
  • Data long-duration Treasuries, gold, broad equity and the volatility index over 5,472 trading days.
  • Model two-state Gaussian hidden Markov model on volatility log-changes, fitted by expectation–maximization with 7 random restarts.
  • Finding the states separate on dispersion, σ 0.048 against 0.124, not on drift — a volatility regime, not a direction call.
  • Rule full equity exposure in the calm state, full Treasury exposure in the stressed state, applied with a one-day lag to avoid look-ahead.
  • Selection two states chosen over the BIC-optimal four on tradeability and stability, with the override argued rather than assumed.

Stochastic volatility calibration and exotic pricing

Calibrate Heston, Bates and CIR to live market data, price a path-dependent payoff under the fitted dynamics, and verify the Monte Carlo engine against an independent transform method.

Design
  • Heston calibrated to a 15-day equity option slice, S0 = $232.90, r = 1.50%.
  • Simulation Euler discretization with full truncation on the variance process, 200,000 antithetic paths.
  • Payoff 20-day arithmetic-average Asian call, with the spot included in the averaging set.
  • Validation an independent Carr–Madan Fourier engine run against the same calibration; a 70-day 95%-moneyness put priced both ways.
  • CIR fitted to a money-market curve across five tenors, then simulated 100,000 paths over 250 days.

EconometricsCointegration & error correction203 quarters, 1959–2009

Non-stationarity, cointegration and error correction

Two macro series trend together for fifty years. Is that a long-run equilibrium relationship or a spurious regression — and if it is real, how fast does the system correct?

Design
  • Data US consumption and output, 203 quarterly observations, in logs and first differences.
  • Order of integration augmented Dickey–Fuller in levels and differences with lag selection; both series I(1).
  • Cointegration Engle–Granger on the OLS residual against tabulated critical values.
  • Dynamics error-correction model, then a vector error-correction model at rank 1 with cointegrating vector and adjustment loadings, plus 20-quarter impulse responses.
  • Diagnostics residual inspection, structural-stability reasoning, and an explicit argument against fitting GARCH at this sample size.

Machine learningReturn forecasting123 months through the 2008 crisis

Return forecasting across seven model families

Can any of seven machine learning families forecast next-month equity excess return well enough to beat the historical average and support a timing rule?

Design
  • Data 2,850 trading days reduced to 123 monthly targets with 12 engineered predictors.
  • Split 24-month training, 36-month walk-forward validation, 63-month out-of-sample window spanning the financial crisis.
  • Models ridge with random Fourier features expanding 6 predictors to 768, k-means, PCR, CART, shrinkage LDA, RBF SVM, MLP across 5 seeds, and a six-model majority vote.
  • Inference Clark–West and Pesaran–Timmermann hand-implemented; circular block bootstrap, block length 6, 2,000 replications.
  • Leakage control scalers, PCA and k-means refit inside the walk-forward loop; short-rate used only as known at time t; full-sample correlations excluded from selection.

Lattice pricing and dynamic replication

Build the pricing stack from first principles on the lattice, then show that the hedge actually funds the payoff — walked down the worst path, node by node.

Design
  • Base case S0 = K = 100, r = 5%, σ = 20%, T = 0.25.
  • Convergence CRR binomial across 10, 25, 50, 100 and 200 steps, benchmarked to the closed-form value; a 150-step trinomial across strikes 90–110.
  • Greeks lattice delta against the analytic value; vega by bump-and-reprice from 20% to 25%.
  • Replication a short European put, S0 = 180, K = 182, r = 2%, σ = 25%, six months, delta-hedged node by node down the worst-case path with the cash account carried forward.
  • Monte Carlo Heston at two correlation levels, Merton jump-diffusion at two intensities, Longstaff–Schwartz for early exercise, and knock-in barriers.

Curve construction and correlation structure

Fit a sovereign yield curve two ways and ask which assumption you are willing to live with — then test whether a PCA pipeline can tell real correlation structure from noise.

Design
  • Curve nine tenors of sovereign bond yields, from 4.847% at one month to 6.924% at twenty-five years.
  • Methods parametric Nelson–Siegel against a natural cubic spline, with the boundary condition and its extrapolation consequence stated explicitly.
  • Null model first PCA run on simulated uncorrelated Gaussians before touching real data, to establish what "no structure" looks like.
  • Real data PCA on Treasury yield changes, then covariance and correlation PCA side by side on thirty large-cap technology equities over 124 trading days.
  • Validation PCA and SVD shown numerically equivalent, with the correct scaling stated: eigenvalues ≈ S²/(n−1), n = 123.

06

Why a quantitative toolkit sits in an operator's hands

I am a co-founder and CxTO, not a career desk quant, and the financial engineering is deliberate rather than decorative. The methods above are the ones that answer the questions an operating company actually faces.

A product decision, a cohort analysis and a derivative valuation share a structure: a signal buried in a dependent series, a model with more freedom than the data supports, and an incentive to report the flattering number. The discipline is identical — hold out a clean sample, know what a null result looks like before you start, and keep the benchmark that beats you in the write-up.

Before Sartori AI I worked on enterprise AI at IBM Watson and Google Vertex AI, and on retail systems at Macy's and Disney. The quantitative training is what connects the modeling to the P&L rather than leaving it as a demo.

Transfers directly

  • Hindsight accountingAny internal metric selected and evaluated on the same data needs the oracle gap deducted before anyone acts on it.
  • IdentificationTwo models that fit equally well with different parameters mean the question is underdetermined, not answered.
  • Regime awarenessDispersion, not direction, is often what changes state — in volatility and in customer behaviour alike.
  • Benchmark honestyIf a simple rule beats the model, the simple rule is the result.
