Sartori AI

Sartori AI is a B2B apparel intelligence platform for mid-market fashion retailers. It builds a structured graph of garments, wardrobes and post-purchase behaviour, and sells the decisions that graph makes possible — starting with returns.

Go-to-marketCurrent wedge

Reverse Intelligence Snapshot

Apparel retailers lose margin to returns they cannot explain. Return reason codes say a customer clicked "doesn't fit." They do not say which garment, which construction decision, or which supplier produced the problem.

The product
  • Snapshot a bounded diagnostic engagement that turns a retailer's own return data into attributed cause, delivered as a scored assessment rather than a dashboard.
  • SRII the Sartori Returns Intelligence Index — a scored framework across assessment dimensions that gives a retailer a comparable baseline and a ranked set of interventions.
  • Audience merchandising, sourcing and vendor compliance — the functions that can act on an attributed cause, not just observe a rate.
  • Wedge logic returns were selected as the entry point from a systematic review of thirteen stages of the apparel lifecycle, scored on market pull, defensibility and build cost.
Why this seat

The returns software category is crowded with platforms that process returns and report rates. Almost nobody sells attributed cause to the people who choose fabrics, grade sizes and sign supplier contracts.

That gap is the thesis. It is also the risk: the buyer is uncontested partly because the function has not historically owned returns, which makes budget creation part of the sale rather than an afterthought.

Stated plainly Sartori is pre-seed and the wedge is under active review. The lifecycle analysis that selected returns is re-run as the market moves, and the platform below was built to survive a change of wedge rather than to serve one.

Pricing, risk and forecasting — with the limitations reported

Derivative pricing, stochastic volatility calibration, time-series econometrics and machine learning applied to real market data. The through-line is not that the models fit. It is that each result is reported with the amount of it that survives scrutiny.

What it covers
  • Pricing CRR binomial and trinomial lattices, Longstaff–Schwartz least-squares Monte Carlo, barrier and Asian payoffs, Greeks by bump-and-reprice.
  • Stochastic volatility Heston calibration with full-truncation Euler, Merton jump-diffusion with drift compensation, Bates, CIR short rates, Carr–Madan Fourier pricing as an independent check.
  • Econometrics ADF unit-root testing, Engle–Granger cointegration, error-correction and vector error-correction models, Gaussian hidden Markov regime detection.
  • Machine learning ridge with random Fourier features, PCR, CART, LDA, SVM, MLP and ensembles, with Clark–West and Pesaran–Timmermann implemented by hand.
The habit worth hiring

An oracle-gap statistic that priced hindsight at 13.08 points of out-of-sample R² on one model, establishing what any backtest must deduct when selection and evaluation share a sample.

A Heston calibration cross-checked against an independent Fourier engine, which returned identical error from different parameters — reported as non-identifiability rather than as success.

An R² of 0.999 reported as a spurious-regression warning rather than a result, with the insignificant error-correction term named as a real problem with the model.

Research & intellectual property

Peer-reviewedICICPE 2026 · Chiang MaiOral presentation

Beyond the Click

A wear-gap framework for measuring whether a recommended garment is actually worn, using doubly-robust estimation to separate the recommendation's causal effect from the selection effect that normally contaminates it.

The problem

Fashion recommendation systems are evaluated on clicks and conversions, both of which are measured at the moment of purchase. The outcome that matters — whether the garment enters regular wear — happens weeks later and is almost never observed.

That gap between purchase signal and wear outcome is what the paper names and attempts to estimate.

The method

Doubly-robust estimation combines an outcome model with a propensity model so that the estimate stays consistent if either one is correctly specified, rather than requiring both.

That property is the reason it is the right tool here: recommendation data is observational, the assignment mechanism is unknown, and a single-model estimate would absorb the selection effect into the treatment effect.
